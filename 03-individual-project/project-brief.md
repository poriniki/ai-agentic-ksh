## The challenge

Pick **one real, recurring task** from your job that costs you real time, and redesign it so that AI does the right parts, you do the right parts, and the result is at least as good as today. Then run it on real work, measure it, and improve it.

This is not "find a clever prompt". It is a **workflow** (Level 4 on the AI Work Ladder) with persistent context (Level 3), clear delegation decisions, and human checkpoints. By the end, you should have something you will keep using, and that a colleague could use too.

## Choosing your task

A good task:
- Repeats at least **monthly** (weekly is better), so you can run it twice before the deadline
- Takes **1+ hour** each time
- Has a clear **output** (a report, a brief, a set of documents, an analysis, an email pack)
- Can be done with data that the **traffic light** allows (or with anonymised/fictional data at the same structure)

Avoid tasks that are mainly about relationships or judgment calls (negotiation, performance reviews) or that need red data.

## What you must do

### Step 1 — Define the problem (Module 5)
Use the **interview-me** prompt to have the AI question you about the task. Turn the result into a one-paragraph problem statement: what the task is, who receives the output, what "good" looks like, how long it takes today, and what's painful about it.

### Step 2 — Map the current process (Module 4)
List the steps as they happen today (5–12 steps) with time per step. Be honest. This is your baseline.

### Step 3 — Redesign with the Delegation Matrix (Module 2)
For each step decide: 🟢 AI does it · 🟡 AI drafts, I review · ⚪ I do it. Add a **check** for every AI step. Remove steps that shouldn't exist. Name at least one **workflow pattern** you use (chaining, routing, parallelisation, orchestrator–workers, evaluator–optimiser).

### Step 4 — Build the setup (Module 3)
- A **Project** with knowledge files and written **instructions**
- A **prompt for each AI step** (6-part brief where it matters), saved so you can reuse them
- A **quality rubric** (3–6 criteria) used in at least one **evaluator step** (critique in a new chat)
- At least **one** of: data analysis on a file, an artifact/canvas tool, research with sources

### Step 5 — Run it twice
Run the full workflow on **two real instances** (e.g. two weeks, two clients, two months of data). After run 1, fix the weakest step before run 2.

### Step 6 — Measure and reflect
- Time: before vs run 1 vs run 2 (**include checking time**)
- Quality: your judgment against the rubric, and if possible the recipient's feedback
- Errors: what your checks caught
- Risks: data classification and what could go wrong

### Step 7 — Present (5 minutes)
1. The task and why it matters (30 s)
2. Before vs after process map (1.5 min)
3. Show one AI step and its check (1.5 min)
4. Results: time, quality, one failure and what you learned (1 min)
5. What you'll keep doing / what a colleague would need to reuse it (30 s)

## Assessment rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Excellent (full points) | Weak (few points) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem & task choice** | 10 | Real, recurring, valuable task; clear problem statement and success definition | Vague or one-off task |
| **Process map & delegation** | 20 | Clear before/after; sensible matrix choices with reasons; a check for every AI step; at least one step removed or merged | Steps vague; everything delegated or nothing delegated; no checks |
| **AI setup (Description)** | 20 | Project with focused knowledge and clear instructions; reusable prompts with examples and constraints | Generic prompts; no Project or instructions |
| **Discernment & evaluation** | 20 | Rubric-based critique in a new chat; specific verification (named numbers/sources); errors documented | "I checked it" with no detail |
| **Results & honesty** | 15 | Two runs; realistic time measures including review; failures explained; improvement between runs | One run or no measurement; only successes reported |
| **Diligence & reuse** | 15 | Data handled per traffic light; risks named; a colleague could reuse the setup from your documentation | Data risks ignored; setup only works for you |

**Bonus (up to +5):** a colleague tried your workflow and you report their feedback.

## Frequently asked

**Can I use fictional data?** Yes, if your real data is amber/red. Keep the same structure and realistic issues. Say clearly in your submission that the data is fictional.

**What if AI doesn't actually save time on my task?** That's a valid result if you show it honestly and explain why. You'll still score well on everything except time saved, and you'll have learned where the frontier is for your work.

**Can I use a tool other than the company's approved one?** No, for anything except green data.
