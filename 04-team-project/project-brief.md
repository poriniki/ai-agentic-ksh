# Team Project — The AI-Assisted Decision Pack

*Course: The AI-Powered Professional*

**Who:** teams of 2–3 people, ideally from different departments
**Due:** next session, [date — fill in]
**Effort:** about 6–8 hours per person over 1–2 weeks
**Present:** 10 minutes + 5 minutes Q&A at the next session
**Submit:** the four deliverables below + a completed [submission-template.md](submission-template.md) to [location — fill in]

## The challenge

Your team has been asked to bring a **recommendation to leadership** on a realistic business question. You have one week and no extra headcount, but you do have AI.

You will produce a **decision pack** that leadership could actually act on, and along the way, you'll design and document **how your team works with AI**: who does what, which steps AI takes, where humans check, and which rules you follow. The second part matters as much as the first: it's practice for managing AI in a real team.

## Choose a scenario

Pick one by [date — fill in] and tell the facilitator. You may adapt any scenario to your real company context, as long as the data stays within the traffic-light rules.

### Scenario A — Launch decision
Leadership is considering launching **[a new product, service, package or market]**. Should we, and how?
- *Marketing lens:* target segment, positioning, launch campaign and channels
- *Business lens:* market size, competitors, customer requirements, go-to-market and operations
- *Finance lens:* pricing options, launch budget, break-even, three scenarios, key risks

### Scenario B — Cost and process redesign
One internal process (e.g. monthly reporting, client onboarding, proposal production, campaign approval) is slow and costly. Redesign it with AI and build the business case.
- *Marketing lens:* impact on speed to market, quality and brand consistency
- *Business lens:* current vs future process map, stakeholders, requirements, change plan
- *Finance lens:* current cost, investment, savings, payback, risks

### Scenario C — Customer retention problem
Customer churn or repeat purchase rates have worsened. Find likely causes and recommend actions.
- *Marketing lens:* customer feedback themes, retention campaign ideas, messaging
- *Business lens:* customer journey, root-cause hypotheses, service/process fixes
- *Finance lens:* value at risk, cost of each action, expected return, priorities

### Scenario D — Your own challenge
A real cross-functional question your team faces. Must be approved by the facilitator by [date]. It must need input from at least two disciplines.

## Required AI workflow

Your team must use **all five stages** below and document how. This makes you practise every module from the session.

| Stage | What happens | Must use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Discovery** | Clarify the question, stakeholders, success criteria and constraints | Interview-me prompt; assumption check; turn into a one-page problem brief |
| **2. Research** | Gather market, competitor, customer or benchmark information | Web search or Research mode; source table; each team member verifies a share of the claims |
| **3. Analysis** | Numbers and structured analysis | Data analysis on a file (real-approved or fictional) and/or an artifact (calculator, scenario model, scoring matrix); key numbers recomputed by a human |
| **4. Drafting** | Write the decision pack | A shared Project (or shared context document if you can't share Projects) with instructions; a 6-part brief for each major section |
| **5. Red team** | Stress-test before presenting | Evaluator–optimiser loop in new chats: at least **two** critic roles (e.g. sceptical CFO, the customer, a competitor, a risk officer); revise based on critique |

**Workflow patterns:** your team must use **parallelisation** (members work on different lenses or different competitors at the same time, then combine) and **evaluator–optimiser** (Stage 5). Name any other patterns you use.

## Deliverables

### 1. Decision pack (max 5 pages or 8 slides)
- The question and the recommendation (first page, in 3 sentences)
- Evidence: research, analysis, numbers (with sources)
- Options considered (at least 2) with pros, cons and key assumptions
- Risks and how to reduce them
- Next steps and what you'd test first
- A short note on what was AI-assisted and how it was checked

### 2. AI workflow diagram (1 page)
A visual of your five stages: for each step, who (which team member), what AI did, which feature/pattern, and where the human checkpoints were. A table is fine.

### 3. Team AI Charter (1–2 pages)
How your team would use AI if this were your permanent way of working. Use `05-resources/ai-team-policy-template.md` as a starting point. It must cover:
- Approved tools and the data traffic light for your work
- Shared Projects: which ones, what goes in them, who owns them
- Your prompt/example library: where it lives, how people add to it
- Review rules: what needs a second human check
- Disclosure: when you say AI helped
- Roles: who is the AI champion, and what they do

### 4. Lessons log (1 page)
- 3 things AI did well in this project
- 3 things it got wrong, and how you caught them
- Where your team disagreed with the AI, and who was right
- What you'd do differently

## Team roles

Assign these (one person can hold two roles in a team of 2):

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Lead** | Owns the timeline and the final decision pack |
| **AI champion** | Sets up the shared Project, keeps the prompt library, writes the AI workflow diagram |
| **Verifier** | Owns the source table and number checks; signs off that every figure in the pack is traced |

Everyone does research, analysis and drafting. The roles define *ownership*, not exclusive work.

## Suggested timeline

| When | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1–2 | Scenario chosen; Discovery done; problem brief agreed; shared Project set up |
| Day 3–5 | Research and analysis in parallel; sources verified |
| Day 6–7 | Draft decision pack; red-team rounds; revise |
| Day 8–9 | AI workflow diagram, Team AI Charter, lessons log |
| Day 10 | Rehearse the 10-minute presentation |

## Presentation (10 minutes + 5 Q&A)

1. The question and your recommendation (1 min)
2. Key evidence and options (3 min)
3. Risks and next steps (1 min)
4. How you worked with AI: the workflow diagram, one example of AI being wrong and how you caught it (3 min)
5. Your Team AI Charter in one slide (2 min)

During Q&A, the audience may play the sceptical leadership team.

## Assessment rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Weak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quality of recommendation** | 20 | Clear, actionable, supported by evidence; options genuinely compared | Vague, generic, or only one option |
| **Evidence & verification** | 20 | Every key number and claim traced to a checked source or recomputed; verification documented | Unsourced numbers; AI claims taken at face value |
| **AI workflow design** | 20 | All 5 stages used thoughtfully; sensible delegation; human checkpoints placed well; parallelisation and evaluator–optimiser clearly applied | Stages skipped or used superficially |
| **Team AI Charter** | 15 | Practical, specific to the team's work, ready to adopt with small changes | Copy of the template; generic |
| **Collaboration** | 10 | Clear roles; shared context used; each member's contribution visible | One person did everything; no shared setup |
| **Reflection & honesty** | 10 | Lessons log shows real errors caught and real disagreements | Only successes reported |
| **Presentation** | 5 | Clear, on time, handles sceptical questions | Over time, unclear |

## Rules

- Data: follow the traffic light. Fictional data with a realistic structure is fine, and must be labelled.
- Every member writes a short paragraph in the submission on what they personally did.
- The recommendation is **yours**. "The AI recommended…" is not an argument; the evidence is.
